# Lunar Odyssey (JAMT): Where the Numbers Come From

This file lists every number the mockup uses and says whether it is **real** (from a NASA or published source), **derived** (calculated from real numbers), or a **game choice** (picked to make the game playable). Track 3 (Data & Science) should check the real ones against the original sources before the pitch.

## Real figures

| Number in the game | Value | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Oxygen per crew member | 0.84 kg/day | NASA *Baseline Values and Assumptions Document* (BVAD) |
| Water to make oxygen | 1.125 kg water → 1 kg O₂ | Chemistry of electrolysis (H₂O → H₂ + ½O₂) |
| Water recovery | ~98% | ISS Environmental Control and Life Support System reached 98% water recovery in 2023 |
| Radiation on the lunar surface | 1.37 mSv/day | Chang'e 4 Lunar Lander Neutron and Dosimetry (LND), Zhang et al., *Science Advances* 2020 |

## Derived figures

| Number | Value | How |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Crew oxygen per day | 3.36 kg | 4 crew × 0.84 kg |
| Water used by the oxygen generator | ~3.8 L/day | 3.36 kg O₂ × 1.125 |
| Net water loss | ~2 L/day | Game choice: a brand-new base recycles worse than the ISS (~93% instead of 98%), which makes water a real worry |
| Extra dose for a 6-hour moonwalk | ~0.2 mSv | 6 h outside at 1.37 mSv/day, minus what the crew would get inside |

## Game choices (not from a source)

These numbers are reasonable guesses picked to make the trade-offs clear. They should be labeled as approximate in the pitch.

- **Power:** 300 kWh battery, 16 kW base load (10 kW in low-power mode) for the bigger outpost, 20 kW from five tall vertical solar arrays (like NASA's planned VSAT towers), 5 kW ridge solar mast, 3 kW greenhouse LEDs, 1 kW extra for the storm shelter.
- **Dark spells:** days 6–8, 15–18 and 24–25. Real rim sites have short dark periods, but this exact schedule is made up.
- **Solar storms:** days 9, 19 and 26, with a one-day warning. Storm doses for one day: 100 mSv outside, about 18 mSv in the habitat (depends on shielding), 2 mSv in the shelter. Real large solar particle events vary a lot. These are in a plausible range but simplified.
- **Habitat shielding:** inside the habitat the dose is 30–60% of the surface dose, depending on how much regolith is piled on top.
- **Greenhouse oxygen:** 0.25 kg/day for a small module.
- **Trips:** energy per trip (10–60 kWh, +20 kWh in the dark), suit oxygen (~1 kg) and cooling water (~8 L), 80 L of water from the ice robot, science points, and the 50%/0% reward for repeat visits.
- **Spacesuit oxygen:** 8 hours per walk plus a 30-minute emergency supply, based on NASA's Artemis Moon suit (AxEMU) design goal of about 8 hours outside. Apollo suits lasted about 7 to 8 hours too. In the game 1 real second = 1 suit minute while walking, so a full tank lasts about 8 real minutes. Hard work uses more oxygen, as on real spacewalks: standing still ×0.8, walking ×1, bounding ×1.4. If it runs out, the crew rescue the walker and any rocks in the bag are lost.
- **Starting reserves:** 42 kg O₂, 320 L water, 21 days of food, 180 kWh battery, 50% shielding, 2 spare parts (harder setting). Science goal: 50 points. About one day in six something breaks (pump, CO₂ fan, power cable, greenhouse pump, rover motor): spend a spare part or take the loss. "Full" reserve bar = 80 kg O₂ / 600 L water.

## Visual choices

- **3D terrain** is generated by code, shaped like Shackleton (a 21 km bowl with a raised rim, a sunlit ridge and small craters). It is not built from real elevation data. A next step could load real LOLA elevation tiles.
- **The base is enlarged** about 10–20× so you can see it. At true scale it would be a speck next to the crater.
- **The Sun is set at 3.5°** instead of the real ~1.5° maximum, and a little fill light is added. Real lunar shadows are almost pitch black, which makes the ground hard to read for young players.
- **The photo map** uses NASA Scientific Visualization Studio item 4716 (credit: NASA SVS / Ernie Wright, from LRO LOLA data).
- **The Luna Prime ending picture** is AI concept art. It is labeled as imagined, not NASA data.

### What you see during flares, and how dust moves
- **Flares:** the Sun is a small, blinding white disc in a black sky (no air to make the sky blue). A flare's light arrives first (about 8 minutes from the Sun), so the Sun flashes when the alert comes in. The fast particles arrive a little later and show up as white specks and streaks on the camera, like the "snow" in NASA SOHO pictures during big storms (Apollo astronauts also saw flashes with their eyes shut). Inside the base you see a few; in the storm shelter, none.
- **Dust:** there is no air, so no wind and no dust clouds. Dust only moves when something kicks it, then flies in a clean arc and drops straight back down in 1/6 gravity: rooster tails behind the rover wheels (as filmed on Apollo 15–17), small sprays from boots, and a flat, fast sheet of grit from the lander's rocket (Apollo 12 / Surveyor 3).
- **Moving on the Moon:** boots press down with only 1/6 of their Earth weight, so there is little grip. Kofi speeds up slowly, skids about 2 m when stopping from a bound, can't steer in the air, is slowed uphill and pulled downhill, and can't climb slopes over 26°. Walk ≈ 1.1 m/s, bound (Shift) ≈ 2.2 m/s, like Apollo crews. Hold Space to crouch, let go to push off: up to about 0.5 m high and 2 s in the air (John Young jumped about 0.4 m on Apollo 16). Turning hard at speed can trip him onto his knees, which cost Apollo astronauts time and effort too. No jetpacks: nobody has used thrusters to move on the Moon's surface.
- **Solid objects:** astronauts can't walk through the base, the rover, the lander, the solar array masts, boulders, furniture or other people.

### Trade-off moments with a 10-second timer
Every emergency call (breakdowns, flare alerts, the lander dust blast) gives reading time, then 10 seconds to decide.
If nobody answers, the worse outcome happens, like in a real emergency. There is no free choice: covering up costs work hours or power,
carrying on costs radiation or equipment.

## Story mode: Lunar Odyssey (the team's 30-day narrative)

The 30-day mission is now the story, open from the start on the mission map. Missions 1–4 are optional training. It comes from the team's `story.json` and story director: 29 beats, 3 checklists and 6 endings.

| Chapter | Days | Places open | Big event |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · A New Beginning | 1–10 | Rim Lookout, Connecting Ridge, landing pad | Day 9 power fault (checklist: 20 kWh, the rest of the day) |
| 2 · Into the Unknown | 11–20 | + Cold Trap Crater (ice), Boulder Field, Fresh Crater | Rover fault on the first trip (checklist, 1 spare part, or the trip is cancelled) |
| 3 · The Final Countdown | 21–30 | all | Storm on Day 22, then the Day 24 sensor swap (1 spare, or the greenhouse controller) |

- **Storms** are on Days 14 and 22, moved off the chapter openings as the plan asks. Dark spells are unchanged.
- **Checklists:** each wrong choice explains itself and costs 10 kWh.
- **Storm warnings** name the latest real solar particle event from NASA DONKI when the game is online. The plan's placeholder event IDs were not shipped, and no event is ever made up.
- **The intro** ends with the base computer's countdown, as asked: "Welcome, astronauts! … 3… 2… 1…"
- **Voices:** cutscenes are spoken in each character's voice (browser speech) with captions always shown, and every one is skippable.
- **Endings:**
  - The win shows liftoff, the central message and a Lunar Explorer Certificate (a canvas you can save or print). The name line is blank, so no child data is collected.
  - The failure endings (power, oxygen, water, radiation) each give a one-line lesson.
- **Judges** can jump to Chapter 1, 2 or 3 from the map. The base is set up as it would be on that chapter's first day.
- **Balance, play-tested** with an automatic player running the real game code (14 Nov 2026): 250 careful games and 400 random games, Cadet track.

  | Player | Wins | Main way to lose | Science goal reached |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Careful, Cadet (goal 50, before training) | 95% | greenhouse lost in Ch. 3, so the harvest and some food are missed | median Day 28 (19–31) |
  | Careful, Specialist (no safety net) | 68% | rover stranded (26%) | median Day 28 |
  | Random, Cadet | ~0% | food (57%) and power (40%) | — |

  (These are the first balance pass, with a goal of 50. Chapter 1 training raised it to 60; see below.) With the chapter locks it lands near the end, where the team's plan wanted it (Days 25–28). Spare parts are the real choice: 3 in total, wanted by the mast, breakdowns, the rover fault, the boulders and the sensor.
- **Fixes the play-test found:**
  - **Cadet safety:** like real rover crews, the rover keeps enough battery to get home. It won't start a drive or job that would leave it stranded, and before the ice robot goes down it plans for the hottest heater setting. Specialists can still take the risk.
  - **The ice run can be aborted** ("winch the robot back up"). Before, a frozen drill with no spare part trapped the rover at the crater.
  - **Rover fault with no spare:** the crew drives home slowly, as the script says, instead of being left out there.
  - **Locked ice steps:** steps that weren't ready could be run again by code. The buttons hid them, but now they're blocked properly.

### Chapter 1 training (Days 1–10)

Chapter 1 used to go quiet after Day 2. Now Mission Control gives one hands-on training task each morning. The team's summary says Chapter 1 is where the crew "activate the base, inspect their equipment, and begin their first scientific experiments".

| Task | System it teaches |
|---|---|
| Switch on the greenhouse grow lights | food and power |
| Build the solar mast on the sunny ridge | power in the dark |
| Collect rock samples at the Rim Lookout | rover trips and science |
| Moonwalk: bring home 3 rocks on foot | spacewalks and suit oxygen |
| Pile regolith bags on the habitat | radiation shielding |
| Practise a storm-shelter drill | storms (before the real one on Day 14) |
| Get through the dark spell on low power | battery and oxygen trade-off |

- **Badges, not science:** each task earns a ⭐ training badge. The first play-test gave science, and trained players reached the goal on Day 12, which emptied Chapters 2 and 3 again.
- **Bonus part:** all 7 badges earn a 3D-printed spare part. The ISS has had a 3D printer since 2014.
- **Certificate:** it shows how many badges you earned.
- **Science goal:** now 60, not 50. Play-tested: careful players who train win 93% and reach 60 around Day 20. Players who skip training reach it about half the time, and still end safely with "Mission incomplete".
