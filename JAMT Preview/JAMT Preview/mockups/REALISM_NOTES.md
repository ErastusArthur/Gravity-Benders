# JAMT Base Mockup: Where the Numbers Come From

This file lists every number the mockup uses and says whether it is **real** (from a NASA or published source), **derived** (calculated from real numbers), or a **game choice** (picked to make the game playable). Track 3 (Data & Science) should check the real ones against the original sources before the pitch.

## Real figures

| Number in the game | Value | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Oxygen per crew member | 0.84 kg/day | NASA *Baseline Values and Assumptions Document* (BVAD) |
| Water to make oxygen | 1.125 kg water → 1 kg O₂ | Chemistry of electrolysis (H₂O → H₂ + ½O₂) |
| Water recovery | ~98% | ISS Environmental Control and Life Support System reached 98% water recovery in 2023 |
| Radiation on the lunar surface | 1.37 mSv/day | Chang'e 4 Lunar Lander Neutron and Dosimetry (LND), Zhang et al., *Science Advances* 2020 |
| Short-term dose limit | 250 mSv over 30 days | NASA-STD-3001: 30-day limit for blood-forming organs is 250 mGy-Eq (treated as mSv in the game) |
| Fission power unit | 10 kW | NASA Kilopower project (KRUSTY test, 2018) covered 1–10 kW units |
| Lettuce growth time | 28 days | NASA Veggie on the ISS: lettuce took about 28–33 days from planting to harvest |
| Shackleton crater size | ~21 km wide, ~4 km deep | LRO LOLA topography |
| Sunlit ridges | lit 80%+ of the year | LRO LOLA illumination studies of the south pole |
| Permanently shadowed crater floor | about −230 °C (~40 K) | LRO Diviner temperature maps |
| Water ice in polar craters | confirmed | LCROSS impact into Cabeus crater, 2009 |
| Sun height at the pole | never more than ~1.5° above the horizon | Moon's axial tilt is ~1.5° |
| Earth near the horizon | always low in the sky at the south pole | Geometry of the Earth–Moon system |
| Mission length | 30 days | In the range NASA describes for Artemis surface stays (Artemis III plans about a week; later base-camp stays are planned to be longer) |

## Derived figures

| Number | Value | How |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Crew oxygen per day | 3.36 kg | 4 crew × 0.84 kg |
| Water used by the oxygen generator | ~3.8 L/day | 3.36 kg O₂ × 1.125 |
| Net water loss | ~0.3 L/day | ~2% loss on ~13–15 L/day of crew water use |
| Extra dose for a 6-hour moonwalk | ~0.2 mSv | 6 h outside at 1.37 mSv/day, minus what the crew would get inside |

## Game choices (not from a source)

These numbers are reasonable guesses picked to make the trade-offs clear. They should be labeled as approximate in the pitch.

- **Power:** 300 kWh battery, 12 kW base load (8 kW in low-power mode), 20 kW solar arrays, 5 kW ridge solar mast, 3 kW greenhouse LEDs, 1 kW extra for the storm shelter.
- **Dark spells:** days 6–8, 15–18 and 24–25. Real rim sites have short dark periods, but this exact schedule is made up.
- **Solar storms:** days 11 and 22, with a one-day warning. Storm doses for one day: 100 mSv outside, about 18 mSv in the habitat (depends on shielding), 2 mSv in the shelter. Real large solar particle events vary a lot. These are in a plausible range but simplified.
- **Habitat shielding:** inside the habitat the dose is 30–60% of the surface dose, depending on how much regolith is piled on top.
- **Greenhouse oxygen:** 0.25 kg/day for a small module.
- **Trips:** energy per trip (10–60 kWh, +20 kWh in the dark), suit oxygen (~1 kg) and cooling water (~8 L), 80 L of water from the ice robot, science points, and the 50%/0% reward for repeat visits.
- **Starting reserves:** 60 kg O₂, 500 L water, 60% shielding, 3 spare parts. "Full" reserve bar = 80 kg O₂ / 600 L water.

## Visual choices

- **3D terrain** is generated by code, shaped like Shackleton (a 21 km bowl with a raised rim, a sunlit ridge and small craters). It is not built from real elevation data. A next step could load real LOLA elevation tiles.
- **The base is enlarged** about 10–20× so you can see it. At true scale it would be a speck next to the crater.
- **The Sun is set at 3.5°** instead of the real ~1.5° maximum, and a little fill light is added. Real lunar shadows are almost pitch black, which makes the ground hard to read for young players.
- **The photo map** uses NASA Scientific Visualization Studio item 4716 (credit: NASA SVS / Ernie Wright, from LRO LOLA data).
- **The Luna Prime ending picture** is AI concept art. It is labeled as imagined, not NASA data.
